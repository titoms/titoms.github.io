## Draft

If you ask ten agencies "how much does an MVP cost," you'll get ten different numbers, and all of them will be technically true. That's not dishonesty — it's because the question is incomplete. Cost isn't a property of the word "MVP." It's a property of the scope behind it, and most founders lock in a budget before that scope actually exists.

Here's the realistic range, what actually moves the number, and why the estimate you got in week one is probably wrong.

### The real range in 2025

Across freelancers, agencies and in-house teams, most MVPs land somewhere between **$10,000 and $150,000**. That's a wide range on purpose — it maps to very different products.

| MVP type | Typical range | Typical timeline | Team model |
|---|---|---|---|
| Landing page + waitlist / single core workflow | $5,000 – $15,000 | 2–4 weeks | Solo freelancer |
| Standard MVP (auth, one core feature, basic dashboard) | $15,000 – $50,000 | 6–12 weeks | Freelancer or small agency |
| Mid-complexity (multi-role, integrations, payments) | $50,000 – $100,000 | 3–5 months | Agency or small in-house team |
| Complex (compliance, AI features, multi-platform) | $100,000 – $250,000+ | 5+ months | Larger team, specialized roles |

Rates vary as much as scope does. Freelancers and Eastern Europe / LATAM teams often run **$20–$60/hour**, Western Europe and North America agencies run **$80–$150/hour**, and specialized senior teams in the US can run **$150–$230/hour**. The same spec can cost 3–4x more depending purely on who builds it.

### What actually drives the price

Five variables explain almost all of the spread between a $15K MVP and a $150K one:

1. **Scope and complexity** — a login form, one core workflow and email notifications is a different job than multi-role permissions, real-time features, or an AI pipeline. Each additional user type, each conditional business rule, each "what happens when X fails" adds hours that don't show up in a one-line feature list.
2. **Team model and location** — freelancer vs. agency vs. in-house, and the region they're based in. This is usually the single biggest lever on the final number, independent of scope.
3. **Stack and platform** — a single web app is cheaper than "web + iOS + Android" from day one. Cross-platform frameworks reduce but don't eliminate the multi-platform premium.
4. **Design depth** — a functional UI using an existing design system is far cheaper than custom UX research, wireframes and a full visual identity before a line of code is written.
5. **Speed** — compressing a 12-week timeline into 6 weeks means more people working in parallel, which means more coordination overhead, which shows up directly in the invoice.

### The hidden costs nobody puts in the first quote

The number in the proposal is rarely the number you actually pay. Costs that routinely get left out of the initial estimate — and typically add **20–40% on top** of the headline figure:

- Project management and coordination overhead
- Infrastructure, hosting, and third-party API costs (auth providers, payment processors, email services)
- Legal and compliance work (terms, privacy policy, GDPR/SOC 2 if relevant)
- QA and bug-fixing passes after "feature complete"
- Post-launch maintenance, which starts the day you ship, not the day you plan a v2

If a quote doesn't mention at least three of these, it's not a complete quote — it's a starting point for a bigger number later.

### Why most MVP estimates are wrong before the build starts

This is the part founders feel but rarely name: the estimate wasn't wrong because the developer lied. It was wrong because **the thing being estimated wasn't fully defined yet.**

A few patterns show up constantly:

- **Vague feature descriptions.** "User dashboard" and "payment integration" sound like features, but they're categories. Which roles see the dashboard? What happens on a failed payment — retry, downgrade, lock the account? Until those are answered, "payment integration" could mean three days or three weeks.
- **No real discovery phase.** Skipping a structured discovery step (even a one-to-two week one) means edge cases surface mid-sprint instead of before the quote — always at the worst possible time.
- **MVP creeping into MMP or MLP.** Somewhere between "minimum viable" and "the version I'd be proud to show my mother," scope quietly triples. Nobody decided that on purpose; it happened one small addition at a time.
- **Scope creep compounding silently.** Individually, each "can we also add..." feels small. Collectively, scope creep adds roughly **25–40% to the original budget** — a $50K MVP finishing at $65–70K is the norm, not the exception.

None of this means estimates are useless. It means an estimate given before scope is fixed is a guess wearing a number's clothes.

### How to scope so the budget actually holds

1. **Run a real discovery phase first**, even a short one. Force answers to the boring questions: how many user types, what happens on every failure path, which integrations are actually required for launch vs. nice-to-have.
2. **Write a feature freeze list**, not a wishlist. Everything on it ships in v1. Everything else goes on a v2 list — written down, not forgotten, just not now.
3. **Separate "minimum viable" from "the version you're proud of."** The MVP's job is to test a hypothesis with real users, not to impress your future customers with polish.
4. **Add a deliberate buffer.** Given that scope creep averages 25–40%, budgeting a flat 20% contingency up front is more honest than pretending it won't happen.
5. **Match the budget to the goal, not the other way around.** A common rule of thumb: your MVP should cost roughly **20–30% of your total first-year budget** — if you have $100K for year one, plan to spend $20–30K validating the idea, and protect the rest for the iteration that follows once you have real user feedback.

### The real answer

"How much does an MVP cost in 2025?" has no single number, and anyone who gives you one before understanding your scope is guessing. The honest answer is: **a well-scoped MVP with a clear feature-freeze list, a defined team model, and an honest 20% buffer usually lands between $15K and $50K for most founders** — and the estimates that miss that range almost always trace back to scope that was never actually locked down.

## FAQ

**Can I build an MVP for under $10,000?**
Yes, if the scope is genuinely minimal — a single core workflow, no multi-role logic, and a freelancer or no-code/low-code approach. It stops being realistic the moment you add auth roles, payments, or multiple integrations.

**Is a fixed-price quote safer than time-and-materials?**
Fixed-price only holds if scope is genuinely frozen before the quote. If requirements are still fluid, time-and-materials with a capped budget and a discovery phase first is usually more honest.

**Why did my MVP cost double what I was quoted?**
The two most common causes are skipped discovery (edge cases surfaced mid-build) and scope creep (small additions that were never re-quoted). Both are avoidable with a feature-freeze list agreed before work starts.

**Should I hire a freelancer or an agency for my first MVP?**
Freelancers are usually cheaper and faster for a genuinely small, single-feature MVP. Agencies make more sense once you need multiple roles (design, backend, QA) coordinated at once, or when reliability and process matter more than the lowest hourly rate.
