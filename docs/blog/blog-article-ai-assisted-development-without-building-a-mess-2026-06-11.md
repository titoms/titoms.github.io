## Draft

AI-assisted development can make a solo founder feel incredibly fast. With ChatGPT, Claude Code, Codex, Stitch or Claude Design, a feature that used to take days can now appear in an afternoon. I saw this evolution clearly between Edumation and Keevo. Edumation was my experimentation project: Cursor, Claude Code, ChatGPT, different stacks and many ways of working. Keevo was built much more deliberately with a modern AI-enhanced workflow from the start.

The danger is not that AI cannot build. The danger is that building becomes so easy that you build everything. Small features feel cheap, so you add dashboards, settings, exports, filters, onboarding screens and edge cases before asking if they matter. When building is easy, you have to think much more about what to build.

The second risk is project understanding. Before AI, a developer knew the app deeply because every feature took hours of work. Now your project can do more than you remember asking for. That is powerful, but it means documentation is no longer optional. You need a clear project index, feature list, architecture notes, decisions and roadmap phases.

My recommendation is simple: slow the project down on purpose. Use AI to compare options, scaffold code, create screens and debug faster, but keep human control over scope. Work in phases: first understand the problem, then define the smallest useful version, then build, then document what changed before moving forward.

AI-assisted development is not a shortcut around product thinking. It is a multiplier. If your scope is clear, it helps you ship. If your scope is messy, it helps you create a bigger mess faster.

## FAQ

**What is AI-assisted development?**  
Using AI tools to support planning, design, coding, debugging and documentation while the human keeps responsibility for product and technical decisions.

**What should solo founders avoid?**  
Do not let every easy feature become a necessary feature. Prioritize the roadmap before prompting the next implementation.
